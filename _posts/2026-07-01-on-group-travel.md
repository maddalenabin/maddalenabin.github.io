title: "Strangers who become friends for a week"
date: 2026-07-01
---

I recently became a WeRoad coordinator. I haven't led a trip yet, my first one is in September, Iceland, nine days in tents. I'm equal parts excited and nervous.

I applied because I've been on the other side of this enough times to know what a good coordinator does, even if I've never been one myself. There's a specific moment that happens on group trips, usually around day three. The group has done one long day together, something physical, something slightly uncomfortable, and in the evening someone orders one too many drinks for the table, and suddenly everyone is talking at the same time and nobody wants to go to sleep.

I want to be the person who helps make that happen.

I also travel a lot on my own. Solo travel is its own thing: you move at your own pace, you make no compromises, you eat what you want when you want. It builds a particular kind of confidence. But it's also quiet in a way that group travel never is.

When you put twelve strangers on a bus in Iceland or in a rice paddy in Vietnam, something strange happens. The shared discomfort, the cold, the wrong turn, the hostel that was nothing like the photos, accelerates connection in a way that years of normal socializing doesn't. You skip all the small talk layers and land somewhere more real, faster.

I've been on the receiving end of that. Now I want to learn how to be on the other side. September will be the test.
